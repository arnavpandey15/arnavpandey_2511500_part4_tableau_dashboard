# Chart Selection Justification

## Overview

The executive dashboard was designed using visualization best practices to ensure that business leaders can quickly understand business performance and make informed decisions. Each chart was selected based on the business question it answers.

---

## Chart Selection

| Dashboard View | Chart Type | Justification |
|----------------|------------|---------------|
| Sales Trend | Line Chart | A line chart is the most appropriate choice for displaying sales trends over time and identifying seasonality or growth patterns. |
| Regional Performance | Horizontal Bar Chart | A horizontal bar chart allows easy comparison of sales and profit across regions and makes ranking straightforward. |
| Category Profitability | Bar Chart | A bar chart clearly compares profitability across product categories and sub-categories. |
| Customer Segment | Bar Chart | A bar chart provides a simple comparison of sales performance across customer segments. |
| Shipping Performance | Bar Chart | A bar chart effectively compares average delivery days across different shipping modes. |
| Discount vs Profit | Scatter Plot | A scatter plot is best suited for analyzing the relationship between discounts and profitability while highlighting outliers. |
| Return Analysis | Bar Chart | A bar chart enables quick comparison of return rates across categories, customer segments, or regions. |

---

## Visualization Design Principles

### Correct Chart Selection

Each visualization was selected to best represent the underlying data and answer a specific business question.

### Clear Hierarchy

The dashboard begins with KPI cards, followed by overall sales trends, and then detailed operational analyses to guide users from summary metrics to deeper insights.

### Minimal Clutter

Unnecessary gridlines, borders, and decorative elements were removed to improve readability and focus attention on the data.

### Consistent Color Usage

A consistent color palette was applied throughout the dashboard:

- Blue for Sales
- Green for Profit
- Red for Losses or High Return Rates
- Orange for Shipping Delays

### Proper Labels

Every chart includes descriptive titles, axis labels, legends (where appropriate), and informative tooltips.

### Readable Titles

Chart titles clearly describe the purpose of each visualization, making the dashboard easy to interpret.

### Appropriate Sorting

Bar charts are sorted in descending order of the relevant metric to improve comparison and highlight top-performing categories or regions.

### Useful Filters

Interactive filters for Region, Category, Customer Segment, Date, Ship Mode, and Campaign Channel allow users to explore the data dynamically.

### Avoidance of Misleading Scales

Charts use appropriate axis scales and formatting to ensure accurate interpretation without exaggerating differences.

### Business Interpretation

Each visualization is designed to answer a practical business question, enabling executives to monitor performance, identify risks, and make informed strategic decisions.

---

## Conclusion

The dashboard applies visualization best practices by selecting appropriate chart types, maintaining a clear visual hierarchy, minimizing clutter, using consistent colors, and focusing on actionable business insights. These design principles make the dashboard suitable for executive reporting and decision-making.
